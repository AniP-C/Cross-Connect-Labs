## Takeaways that still apply

- **The gap is the mix.** AI companies look modern but stop at business software. Grid companies are credible but heavy and corporate. Nobody friendly covers software *and* electrical work. The patch bay design is built on that gap.
- **Show proof, not adjectives.** The strongest sites name customers and give numbers. Replace the example case studies with real ones and measured results.
- **Show the work itself:** workflow diagrams (n8n), the bot inside a chat (Moveworks, Workato), engineering notes and diagrams (SEL).
- **Name things.** Services firms name their methods and stages (Tribe AI: Map, Build, Activate; Thoughtworks: AI/works). The site does this with equipment names: rack modules, signal path, spec sheet, field reports.
- **Colour space.** Teal and lime are crowded among energy and automation brands. The copper from your logo stands out.
- **Market signals.** Grid companies are moving toward power for AI data centres, and "cross-connect" is a data-centre term. Siemens leads its homepage with an AI agent for automation engineering. This could be a later service page if you take on that work.

## How the direction changed

1. **First brief:** a company doing AI automation, agentic AI pipelines and grid electrical automation, with Slack support.
2. **Second brief:** the Slack focus was dropped. A freelancer, with AI workflows first and some electrical work.
3. **Final brief:** one person. Websites, apps, online stores with admin panels, WhatsApp bots, a doctor bot with CRM, AI workflows, small freelance jobs and some electrical automation. No minimum budget.
4. **Design:** after several rounds of mockups (see [design.md](design.md)), Option B day/night was chosen. It uses the logo's copper and navy instead of the amber and teal the report first suggested.
